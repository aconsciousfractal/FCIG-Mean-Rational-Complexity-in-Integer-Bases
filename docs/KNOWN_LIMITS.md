# Limits

- The additive optimum C* and worst finite lengths are unknown.
  Finite-base reduction does not prove finite-length attainment.
- The sign is not classified for all bases with at least three primes.
  B30,N2 is a finite positive example; radical510510 has seven primes.
- No explicit complete mixed-base limiting law, certified mixed log-moment
  constants or effective finite-to-limit rate is proved in this article.
- The full-unit denominator condition differs from a single divisibility
  exclusion, unit-state sampling and some FCSR connection conventions.
- The two-prime proof uses executable finite premises and written infinite
  arguments. All consumed computational stages have now been regenerated in
  normal and optimized modes. Every central aggregate threshold and proper
  gcd stratum has passed a distinct enumeration. All 4,596 semiprime
  histograms and consumed ledgers are distributed and checked.
- The stronger global bound adds finite geometric premises beyond the original
  two-prime chain. Retained arithmetic checks, full source reconstructions,
  independent scalar recomputation and sampled alternative geometric audits
  have separate scopes. None constitutes formal or human peer verification.
- The short baseline and complete-evidence commands retain their explicitly
  stated scopes. The latter validates recorded central coverage but does not
  repeat the enumeration; use the full reconstruction command to rerun it.
- Far histograms dominate the true height. Their domination still uses the
  written proof. No true far-minimum vector is claimed for aggregate premises.
- Source/version comparison does not certify priority. The final
  Chen–Winterhof journal body was not read.
- AI-assisted reviews share exposure. They are not independent human
  peer review or formal proof verification.
- Runtime observations do not impose a RAM bound, bit-complexity theorem
  or scalability guarantee. Hosted CI was not observed running.
  No universal FCSR equivalence or Collatz consequence is asserted.
